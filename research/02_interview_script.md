# Step 2: Unbiased Interview Script

**Duration:** 45 to 55 minutes. **Format:** one-on-one, recorded with consent.

## Interviewer Rules

1. **Never name the product, the company, or any price until Q12.** If the subject asks what you are building, say: "I'd rather not bias you. I'll tell you everything at the end and you can tell me what you think of it then." This is not evasion, it is the method.
2. **No yes/no constructions.** Every question opens with what, how, tell me, walk me through, or describe. If a question can be answered "yes," it is broken.
3. **No leading premises.** Do not say "frustrating," "confusing," "hidden," "too expensive," "painful," or "opaque" before the subject says it. You are testing whether they reach for those words unprompted. If you supply the word, the data is gone.
4. **Ask for the last time, not the usual.** Specific recalled episodes are evidence. Generalizations are self-presentation. "What do you usually do" invites a flattering answer; "walk me through the last time" does not.
5. **Silence after the answer.** Count to five. The second thing they say is the true thing.
6. **Record the vocabulary verbatim.** Their nouns matter more than their sentiment. Write down the exact words for fees, taxes, and advisors.
7. **Let them be uninterested.** A subject who shrugs is giving you the most valuable answer in the study. Do not rescue the question by rephrasing it more attractively.

## Section 1: Warm-Up (1 question, ~6 min)

### Q1 — Career, asset layout, and peace of mind
> "Tell me about your career over the last five years or so, and as you go, walk me through where your money ended up living. Then I want to know: when you think about all of it together, what's your gut feeling?"

**Purpose:** establishes the account map, the trigger-event history, and an unprompted baseline emotional read before any topic is introduced.

**Probes:**
- "You mentioned [account] almost in passing. What's the story with that one?"
- "When you say [their exact word for the feeling], what's behind that?"
- "Which of those accounts do you think about least?"

**Listen for:** stranded accounts named apologetically. Equity comp mentioned as a separate mental category from savings. Whether the gut feeling is about *amount*, *complexity*, or *uncertainty*. Which of the three they name first is the pain hierarchy.

## Section 2: Core Questions (10 questions, riskiest assumptions first, ~35 min)

### Q2 — `ka-005` Pricing, `ka-003` Fee opacity | Cost discovery behavior
> "Walk me through the last time you found out what something in your financial life was costing you. What were you doing when you found out?"

**Why first:** pricing is the riskiest assumption in the plan and the one most easily flattered later by a founder asking "would you pay." Asking it now, as a behavioral question with no price on the table, gets an uncontaminated read on whether cost is even a live object in their mind.

**Probes:**
- "Was that something you went looking for, or did it find you?"
- "What did you do with that information?"
- "What do you pay now, across everything?" (Ask for the number. Then ask how they know it.)

**Listen for:** whether they can produce a dollar figure, a percentage, or nothing. Whether the discovery was self-initiated or accidental. Whether learning the cost changed any behavior, because a fee that produces no action is not a pain, it is a grievance.

**Do not say:** "hidden fees," "expense ratio," "how much are they taking."

---

### Q3 — `ka-003` Primary pain | Unranked problem inventory
> "If you could hand off one part of managing your money to someone else and never think about it again, which part would you pick? And then tell me what you'd pick second."

**Probes:**
- "What makes that the first one?"
- "What's the part you'd absolutely keep for yourself?"
- "Where does [a pain they named earlier in Q1 or Q2] sit on that list?"

**Listen for:** the rank order. `ka-003` claims opacity and jargon are the *primary* pain. This question is the direct test, and it is a genuine test because fee opacity has to beat cash-flow anxiety, college funding, income volatility, and time scarcity to win. Note whether fees appear at all.

**Do not say:** anything that offers a menu of candidate pains.

---

### Q4 — `ka-001` Trust in automation | Revealed delegation history
> "Tell me about something in your financial life that happens automatically right now, without you doing anything. How did it come to be set up that way?"

**Probes:**
- "How do you know it's working?"
- "When did you last check?"
- "What's something you've deliberately kept manual?"

**Listen for:** existing automation is the best available proxy for `ka-001`, and the gap between "I have automation" and "I have verified my automation" is the real finding. Someone who cannot say how they know it works has *convenience tolerance*, not trust, and convenience tolerance will not survive a market drawdown.

---

### Q5 — `ka-001` Trust in automation | The failure boundary
> "Describe a time a system or a company did something with your money that you didn't expect. What happened next?"

**Probes:**
- "What did you want to happen instead?"
- "Who did you want to talk to?"
- "What would have made it acceptable?"

**Listen for:** this question finds the ceiling on `ka-001`. Two distinct answers exist and they require different products: some people want the automation to be *inspectable* (show me the rule), others want it to be *accountable* (give me a person). Record which. A subject who wants a phone number is not a trust failure, they are a support-model requirement.

---

### Q6 — `ka-002` Switching friction | Recalled transfer episode
> "Tell me about the last time you moved money between institutions, or decided not to. Take me through it from the beginning."

**Probes:**
- "How long did the whole thing take, start to finish?"
- "What was the worst part?"
- "What were you worried would go wrong?" (Only if they raise worry themselves.)

**Listen for:** whether the friction they name is *mechanical* (forms, days, paperwork, ACATS), *cognitive* (I didn't know what to move where), or *emotional* (I was afraid it would vanish mid-transfer). `ka-002` assumes mechanical. If the answer is cognitive or emotional, reducing transfer steps does not unblock them and the assumption is invalidated even though the friction is real.

**Do not say:** "ACATS," "transfer process," or any suggestion that transfers are difficult.

---

### Q7 — `ka-002` Switching friction | The stranded account
> "Earlier you mentioned [account]. What would have to happen for that to move?"

**Probes:**
- "How long has it been sitting there?"
- "What's stopped it so far?"
- "Who would you expect to do the work of moving it?"

**Listen for:** the honest reason, which is usually not friction. Frequently it is that nothing forces the issue. If the answer is "nothing has to happen, I just haven't," then the binding constraint is the absence of a deadline, not the presence of friction, and the go-to-market implication is trigger-event timing rather than onboarding UX.

---

### Q8 — `ka-004` Tax value | Last April, reconstructed
> "Take me through last tax season. What did you do, who did you talk to, and what surprised you?"

**Probes:**
- "What did the whole thing cost you, between fees and time?"
- "What did you find out too late to do anything about?"
- "What did you promise yourself you'd do differently this year?"

**Listen for:** whether a tax surprise exists at all, and whether it is a *securities* surprise (loss harvesting, wash sales, lot selection) or something else entirely (withholding, AMT, ISO exercise, real estate depreciation, self-employment). `ka-004` only survives if the surprise sits inside what portfolio automation can reach. A subject whose assets are overwhelmingly tax-deferred has no surface for this value proposition, and that is a clean invalidation, not a soft one.

---

### Q9 — `ka-004` Tax value | The workaround
> "Describe how you keep track of your investment holdings for tax purposes. Show me the actual thing if you have it open."

**Probes:**
- "Who built that?"
- "How many hours a year does it take?"
- "What does it not do that you wish it did?"
- "Tell me about a time it got something wrong."

**Listen for:** the existence and sophistication of a workaround is the single best predictor of willingness to pay in this study, because a workaround is a revealed price already being paid in hours. No workaround means either no pain or no awareness, and those two are very different customers. Quantify the hours and the error history.

---

### Q10 — `ka-006` Onboarding autonomy | Last signup, reconstructed
> "Think of the last financial account you opened. Walk me through how you got from deciding to done."

**Probes:**
- "Where did you stop or put it aside?"
- "What made you pick it up again?"
- "At what point, if any, did you want to talk to a person?"
- "Tell me about a time you were asked to connect one account to another service."

**Listen for:** the abandonment point, and the exact language around credential sharing and account linking. `ka-006` fails in two distinguishable ways: *security refusal* (I don't share logins) and *data-ownership refusal* (I don't want a company holding my balance sheet). These need different answers, and neither is solved by a better flow. Record which one you are hearing.

**Do not say:** "aggregation," "link your accounts," or "it only takes a few minutes."

---

### Q11 — `ka-007` Decision-making unit | Revealed approval process
> "Think of the last financial decision in your household over a few thousand dollars. Tell me who was involved and how it actually got decided."

**Probes:**
- "Who raised it first?"
- "What would have happened if [other person] had said no?"
- "What did they want to know that you hadn't thought about?"
- "Where does the two of you talking about this actually happen?"

**Listen for:** the difference between *consultation* and *approval*. A stated veto that has never been exercised is not a veto. Critically, record the co-approver's *question*, not just their existence: `ka-007` is only actionable if you know what the second person needs to hear, and it is usually institutional legitimacy ("who are these people, what if they fail") rather than anything about fees or performance.

---

## Section 3: Closing Willingness-to-Pay and Value Benchmark (1 question, ~8 min)

### Q12 — `ka-005` Pricing and value capture
Ask in three parts, in this order, and do not skip to part (c).

**(a) Their benchmark, before any number from you:**
> "Set aside what anything costs today. For the work we've been talking about, what would feel like a fair annual price to you, and how did you arrive at that?"

**(b) Structure preference, without amounts:**
> "Some people pay a flat annual amount for this kind of thing, some pay a share of what they have invested, and some pay nothing and do it themselves. Tell me how you think about those three as structures, setting the amounts aside entirely."

**(c) Only now, the actual candidates:**
> "Here's what I've been working on: [one plain sentence, no jargon, no adjectives]. Two ways it could be priced. Ninety-six dollars a year, flat. Or twenty-five basis points of what you have with us, which at your balance is about $[compute it out loud, in dollars, right now]. React to both."

**Probes:**
- "Which of those two would make you less likely to sign up, and why?"
- "What's the number where you'd stop and think about it?"
- "What would you need to see before you paid anything at all?"
- "What would you cancel to pay for this?"

**Listen for:** the reaction to the *structure* is more informative than the reaction to the amount. Watch specifically for three patterns. A subject who prefers the flat fee at a balance where flat is *more expensive* is telling you the objection is principled, not economic. A subject who finds $96 suspiciously cheap is telling you the price is signaling low capability. And a subject who accepts 25 bps without converting it to dollars has not actually priced it, so always do the arithmetic out loud, because an unconverted percentage is not a validated price.

**Do not:** defend the price, explain the value again, or mention the 1% incumbent unless they raise it. If they say no, ask what would have to be different and then stop.

## Assumption Coverage Map

| Assumption | Primary question | Corroborating | Invalidation condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ka-001` Trust in automation | Q4, Q5 | Q1 | Subject cannot verify existing automation, or requires a named human for accountability |
| `ka-002` Switching friction / ACATS | Q6, Q7 | Q1 | Friction named is cognitive or emotional rather than mechanical, or nothing forces the move |
| `ka-003` Opaque fees and jargon as primary pain | Q3 | Q2 | Fees rank below time, cash flow, or comprehension in Q3, or jargon reads as competence |
| `ka-004` Tax optimization value | Q8, Q9 | Q2 | No workaround exists, assets are predominantly tax-deferred, or complexity exceeds automation scope |
| `ka-005` Pricing and 25 bps capture | Q12 | Q2, Q3 | Flat fee preferred at balances where flat costs more, or percentage rejected on structure |
| `ka-006` Onboarding autonomy | Q10 | Q6 | Credential or data-ownership refusal, or subject wants a person at the linking step |
| `ka-007` Spousal co-approver | Q11 | Q1 | Approval is consultation only, or the co-approver's question is unanswerable from the current positioning |

## What This Script Deliberately Does Not Do

- It does not ask whether anyone would use the product. Stated intent at this stage is noise.
- It does not describe the solution before Q12, which means Q2 through Q11 cannot be contaminated by it.
- It does not ask subjects to rank the seven assumptions. They do not know what the assumptions are and should not.
- It does not offer the 1% AUM incumbent as a comparison anchor, because doing so manufactures the fee grievance the study is supposed to be measuring.
