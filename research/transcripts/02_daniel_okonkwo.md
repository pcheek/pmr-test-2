**Interviewer:** Which of those two would make you less likely to sign up, and why?

## Post-Interview Assessment
*(interviewer notes, not the subject speaking)*

### Assumption verdicts

**`ka-001` Trust in Automation — Partially Validated.** He delegates everything and wants no human in the loop ("if a person had appeared I would have been annoyed"), but he cannot verify any of it: "I don't know that it's working. I know that I haven't received an email telling me it's broken." This is convenience tolerance, not trust, and it has never been stress-tested by a drawdown — his stated high risk tolerance is explicitly a function of a small balance.

**`ka-002` Switching Friction / ACATS — Invalidated as stated, with a caveat.** The binding constraint is the absence of a deadline, not mechanical cost: "Nothing's stopped it. There's no obstacle... there is no consequence for not doing it." A secondary blocker is cognitive (rollover destination, pro-rata rule), not procedural. Reducing transfer steps does not unblock him; only doing the transfer *for* him does, which is a done-for-you service assumption rather than a friction-reduction assumption.

**`ka-003` Opaque Fees and Jargon as Primary Pain — Invalidated.** Fees did not appear in his Q3 ranking at all and he refused to place them: "you asked me what I'd hand off and I'm not going to pretend fees are my problem. My problem is a tab." He decoded every piece of jargon in the call without friction and was unimpressed rather than suspicious; he had to be walked through converting 25 bps to dollars on a portfolio he has held for three years.

**`ka-004` Tax Optimization Value — Invalidated.** No workaround exists in any form ("There's nothing to show you"), his only harvestable surface is $26K of a $95K portfolio, tax season costs him $135 and ninety minutes, and he treats loss harvesting as an included feature of the category rather than a benefit worth paying for. He read a $1,900 realized-loss line as proof the product works without knowing what it saved him. Note for the record: at $26K he is likely below the threshold at which his current provider runs TLH at all, and he does not know this — the interviewer did not correct him. A value proposition he already believes he gets for free cannot be sold to him.

**`ka-005` Pricing and 25 bps Capture — Partially Validated, and for the wrong reason.** He accepts 25 bps readily ($222 on $89K, $307 on $123K) and explicitly prefers it over the cheaper $96 flat: "the ninety-six... is the one that makes me hesitate." His reasons are familiarity ("twenty-five bips is just the price of a robo"), capability signaling ("my instinct is 'what's missing'"), and deliberate fee invisibility ("if you take it out of the portfolio I will literally never think about it again"). The number is capturable from him; the incentive-alignment positioning that justifies it is not what captures it, and he volunteered that a visible flat charge is a churn risk in a subscription purge.

**`ka-006` Onboarding Autonomy — Validated, strongly.** Last account opened in four minutes on a phone at 11pm, no abandonment point, no desire for a human at any step, and a dozen prior Plaid links with the screens unread: "I don't read the screens. I don't." He is the cleanest self-serve case in the cohort and adds a hard constraint — a scheduled call in the flow is an instant abandon.

**`ka-007` Spousal Co-approver — Invalidated.** Fully separate finances with a long-term partner, no joint account, rent split 50/50 and nothing else pooled. Investment decisions are unilateral and not even framed as shared: "it wouldn't occur to either of us that she has a vote." No second signature exists on anything.
