## Q12 — Willingness to pay

### (b) Structure preference

**Interviewer:** Some people pay a flat annual amount for this kind of thing, some pay a share of what they have invested, and some pay nothing and do it themselves. Tell me how you think about those three as structures, setting the amounts aside entirely.

**Kate:** Do it yourself is off the table and I want to say why, since I imagine some of your subjects pick it. It's not that I can't. It's that in 2019 I did it myself and it cost me nineteen thousand dollars, and the lesson I took wasn't "learn more," it was "this is a domain where being eighty percent right is the same as being wrong." So free is not cheap for me. Free is how I lost the money.

Percentage of assets. I understand why people dislike it and I think the dislike is mostly aesthetic. The real argument against it is that my cost goes up as the market goes up while the work stays identical, which is genuinely a little absurd. The real argument for it is that it's the only structure where the provider gets hurt when I get hurt, and that alignment is worth something to me. Also, practically, it scales down — if my balance is small, my bill is small, and I didn't have to negotiate.

Flat fee. Cleanest. I know the number in advance, I can budget it, nobody's incentive changes when the market moves. But I'd watch it carefully for what it implies. ### Assumption verdicts

**`ka-001` Trust in Automation — INVALIDATED (as specified).**
She trusts automation within a bounded scope ("software does that... fair price is small") but explicitly requires a named accountable human above that boundary — "what I pay $1,400 for is not a return... it's someone to call in October and someone to blame in April" — and has refused a standing sell-on-vest instruction twice for exactly this reason. Per the coverage map, requiring a named human for accountability is the stated invalidation condition. Note she *can* verify her automation (reads the quarterly PDF, reconciles the 529 debits), so the failure is accountability, not inspectability.

**`ka-002` Switching Friction / ACATS — INVALIDATED.**
Her 2021 transfer cost her forty minutes and she named no worst part: "the transfer itself was the easiest financial thing I've done in ten years... somebody whose job it was did it for me." The stranded ESPP has sat six years with zero mechanical obstacle — "nothing has stopped it; nothing has started it, which is different" — so the binding constraint is absence of a deadline, not friction. Reducing transfer steps removes a cost her incumbent already absorbs, and she names that absorption as part of what the 1% buys.

**`ka-003` Opaque Fees and Jargon as Primary Pain — INVALIDATED.**
She ranked equity-comp tax decisions first and college funding second, and placed fees below both and below the kitchen remodel: "the fee isn't a pain, it's a price, and I made a decision about it with the numbers in front of me." She knew the 1% from signing day; the only new information was multiplication. Jargon is neutral to positive — she is fluent in dense regulatory language and reads agreements professionally.

**`ka-004` Tax Optimization Value — INVALIDATED on scope, validated on value.**
Tax relief is unambiguously a paid-for benefit in her life ($1,400 CPA, willing to go to $2,000), but the complexity that carries the value sits outside portfolio automation: ISO exercise windows, AMT crossover, a 2019 AMT credit carryforward, and RSU basis correction against the broker's 1099.
